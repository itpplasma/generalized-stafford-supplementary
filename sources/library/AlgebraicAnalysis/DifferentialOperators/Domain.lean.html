Dk​(R) is a domain when R is a domain

Let k be a field of characteristic zero and R a commutative k-algebra which is a domain. Then the ring Dk​(R) of finite-order differential operators is a domain (DifferentialOperators.isDomain). No finiteness, smoothness or coordinate hypothesis is needed.

Proof: polarization of the principal symbol

Write ad_b P = [P, b] = P b - b P. The maps adb​ commute pairwise, are additive in b, and satisfy the Leibniz rule adb​(PQ)=Padb​Q+(adb​P)Q (commutatorMap_mul), hence adbN​(PQ)=∑(Nchoosei)adbi​P⋅adbN−i​Q (commutatorMap_pow_mul). If P has order at most m, then adbm​P is multiplication by an element σP​(b)∈R, the principal symbol of P evaluated on db.

  1. If P has exact order m, then σP​(b)=0 for some b (exists_symbol_ne_zero). By induction: some adc​P has exact order m−1, so σadc​P​(b)=0 for some b; the value σP​(b+tc) is a polynomial in t∈k (exists_polynomial_symbol, binomial theorem for the commuting adb​, adc​) whose t-coefficient is mσadc​P​(b)=0. A nonzero polynomial over the domain R has finitely many roots and k is infinite.
  2. If P, Q have exact orders m, n, then adbm+n​(PQ)=(m+nchoosem)adbm​P⋅adbn​Q, which is multiplication by (m+nchoosem)σP​(b)σQ​(b). Choosing b on a line through points where σP​ and σQ​ do not vanish makes both nonzero at once, again since a nonzero polynomial has finitely many roots. Hence PQ=0.

This is the classical fact that the principal symbol is multiplicative and grDk​(R) embeds into polynomial functions on ΩR/k​; compare [McConnell–Robson, Noncommutative Noetherian Rings, 15.5.5]. The polarization argument is AA-native.

8/-!
9# `D_k(R)` is a domain when `R` is a domain
10
11Let `k` be a field of characteristic zero and `R` a commutative `k`-algebra
12which is a domain. Then the ring `D_k(R)` of finite-order differential
13operators is a domain (`DifferentialOperators.isDomain`). No finiteness,
14smoothness or coordinate hypothesis is needed.
15
16## Proof: polarization of the principal symbol
17
18Write `ad_b P = [P, b] = P b - b P`. The maps `ad_b` commute pairwise, are
19additive in `b`, and satisfy the Leibniz rule
20`ad_b (P Q) = P ad_b Q + (ad_b P) Q` (`commutatorMap_mul`), hence
21`ad_b^N (P Q) = ∑ (N choose i) ad_b^i P · ad_b^{N-i} Q`
22(`commutatorMap_pow_mul`). If `P` has order at most `m`, then `ad_b^m P` is
23multiplication by an element `σ_P(b) ∈ R`, the principal symbol of `P`
24evaluated on `db`.
25
261. If `P` has exact order `m`, then `σ_P(b) ≠ 0` for some `b`
27 (`exists_symbol_ne_zero`). By induction: some `ad_c P` has exact order
28 `m - 1`, so `σ_{ad_c P}(b) ≠ 0` for some `b`; the value `σ_P(b + t c)` is a
29 polynomial in `t ∈ k` (`exists_polynomial_symbol`, binomial theorem for the
30 commuting `ad_b`, `ad_c`) whose `t`-coefficient is `m σ_{ad_c P}(b) ≠ 0`.
31 A nonzero polynomial over the domain `R` has finitely many roots and `k` is
32 infinite.
332. If `P`, `Q` have exact orders `m`, `n`, then
34 `ad_b^{m+n} (P Q) = (m+n choose m) ad_b^m P · ad_b^n Q`, which is
35 multiplication by `(m+n choose m) σ_P(b) σ_Q(b)`. Choosing `b` on a line
36 through points where `σ_P` and `σ_Q` do not vanish makes both nonzero at
37 once, again since a nonzero polynomial has finitely many roots. Hence
38 `P Q ≠ 0`.
39
40This is the classical fact that the principal symbol is multiplicative and
41`gr D_k(R)` embeds into polynomial functions on `Ω_{R/k}`; compare
42[McConnell–Robson, *Noncommutative Noetherian Rings*, 15.5.5]. The
43polarization argument is AA-native.
44-/
